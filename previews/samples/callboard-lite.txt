The terrace is colder than the ballroom by a full season. Behind Eleanor the quartet has begun the next set without her, and she can feel, as surely as the draught at her ankles, every head that turned when she slipped out through the glass doors.

Lord Ravensworth does not turn round. He has taken his gloves off, which no gentleman does at Lady Fairfax's, and is resting both bare hands on the cold stone as if he means to push the whole of Grosvenor Square away from him.

"You dropped this, my lord." She holds the letter out between two fingers, the way one holds something that might be hot. The seal is broken. *He knew the hand. He went white when he saw it fall.*

"Did I." Not a question. He looks at the letter, then at her, and his mouth does something that is almost a smile and nothing like one. "And you read it, Miss Ashby."

"I did not." A pause, which she dislikes herself for. "I read the direction. It was addressed to a lady who has been dead these five-and-twenty years."

He takes the letter. His fingers do not touch hers, and somehow that is worse.
